Introduction Organochlorine pesticides (OCPs), regarded as well-known persis- tent organic pollution, have been extensively used in agriculture for control of vector-borne diseases ( Fang et al., 2018 ; Li et al., 2018 ). China has a long history of production and usage of OCPs, and became the largest producer country between 1945 and 1983 ( Li et al., 2018 ). Although OCPs were banned in 2004, they can be still detected fre- quently at high concentrations in the environment such as air, soil water and even food because of their ubiquity, high persistence and resistance to degradation ( Tsygankov et al., 2015 ; Sun et al., 2016; Chang, 2017; Zeng et al., 2018). Furthermore, OCPs could be accu- mulated in animals as well as humans through food chain, and have great potential risk to human health ( Zhao et al., 2017 ; Fang et al., 2018). Among various OCPs, pentachloronitrobenzene (PCNB) is con- sidered as a typical organochlorine fungicide, which has been ex- tensively used as seed dressing and soil treatment for prevention and control of many fungal diseases on wide range of crops in agricultural management (Li and Yang, 2013; Teng et al., 2017 ; Liang et al., 2017).
